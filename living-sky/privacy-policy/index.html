Privacy policy

Weather: Living Sky, by Nikhil. Last updated October 22, 2026.

Weather: Living Sky ("the app") is an Android weather app with home-screen widgets, made by Nikhil ("we", "us"). This policy explains what information the app uses, where it goes, and what you can do about it.

The short version

  • No account and no sign-in. No ads, no usage analytics and no tracking.
  • To show the weather, the app sends a location (yours, if you allow it, or a city you searched for) to the weather services listed below. They use it to answer that request.
  • If the app crashes or stops responding, it sends us a report so we can fix the problem. A report never contains your location or anything that identifies you.
  • Everything else the app remembers stays in the app's private storage on your phone.

What the app uses, and why

Your location (only if you allow it)

If you give the app location permission, it asks Android (through Google Play services) where the phone is. It uses this location to:

  • get your forecast: the latitude and longitude, to four decimal places (about 11 metres), are sent to Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com). For places in Europe, the same location is also sent to Open-Meteo's air-quality service for pollen levels.
  • get official weather alerts: for places in or near the United States, the same location is sent to the US National Weather Service (api.weather.gov).
  • name the place: the location is passed to Android's built-in address lookup to find the town and state. On most phones this lookup is provided by Google.

The app never reads your location in the background. Home-screen widgets use the last location the app saw while it was open.

You can use the app without sharing your location at all: search for a city instead.

City searches

When you search for a city, the text you type is sent to Open-Meteo's place-search service to find matching places. The last five cities you picked are kept on your phone so you can pick them again. You can clear them with "Clear" in the search screen.

Crash reports

If the app crashes, or (on Android 11 and later) stops responding, it keeps a short report on your phone and sends it to us the next time the app runs with an internet connection, so we can find and fix the problem. A report contains:

  • where in the app's code the problem happened (a "stack trace"), and Android's short description of it;
  • the app's version, the Android version, and the phone's make and model;
  • the time it happened.

It contains no location, no city, no account and nothing else that identifies you: before sending, the app removes anything that looks like a location or a web address's details. The report travels over HTTPS to a small Google Apps Script in our Google account, which emails it to us at weatherbynikhil@gmail.com. Like any internet request, it reaches Google with your phone's IP address; we do not see that address. The app sends reports at most three times a day. We keep reports only as long as we need them to fix the problem, and we never sell or share them.

Information that is stored on your phone

The app keeps the following in its private storage, which other apps cannot read:

  • your settings: units, which forecast values to show, animation choices, and each widget's setup;
  • the city you picked, your last five picked cities, and the last device location the app saw (so widgets can refresh without asking for your location again);
  • the most recent forecast, including the place's name and coordinates, so the app and widgets have something to show offline;
  • crash reports waiting to be sent, the times reports were sent (so that they go out no more than three times a day), and the last crash, which you can copy or delete in Settings;
  • pictures of the buildings drawn in the animated scene (no personal information).

It is deleted when you clear the app's storage in Android settings or uninstall the app.

Backups. On Android 12 and later, your settings (including the last device location and recent cities) are included in your Google backup only when that backup is end-to-end encrypted with your phone's screen lock, and they are copied when you move directly to a new phone. The saved forecast and crash reports are never backed up. On Android 11 and earlier, nothing that contains a location is backed up.

Who receives information

We do not sell, rent or share information with anyone. The app talks directly to these services, which also see your phone's IP address as part of any internet connection:

  • Open-Meteo (Switzerland) receives the coordinates of the place you are viewing and your city searches. Its terms are at open-meteo.com/en/terms; Open-Meteo says it keeps web server logs, which may contain coordinates, for up to 90 days, and does not link them to identities.
  • The US National Weather Service receives the coordinates of places in or near the United States. Its privacy policy is at weather.gov/privacy.
  • Google, through Google Play services on your phone, handles location requests and the address lookup as part of how Android provides location. Google also carries crash reports to us (Google Apps Script and Gmail). Its privacy policy is at policies.google.com/privacy.
  • We receive crash reports, as described above.

If you choose to share usage and diagnostics with Google on your phone, Google Play may also give us anonymous, combined crash and performance statistics for the app. That is covered by Google's privacy policy.

Security

All connections use HTTPS, and the app refuses unencrypted connections. The app has no account or password to steal.

Your choices

  • Allow, limit to "approximate", or remove location access at any time in Android settings (Settings > Apps > Weather: Living Sky > Permissions).
  • Use a searched city instead of your location.
  • Clear recent searches in the app, or clear all of the app's data in Android settings.
  • Uninstall the app to remove everything it stored on your phone.
  • Ask us to delete the crash reports your phone sent by writing to weatherbynikhil@gmail.com. Because reports do not identify you, tell us roughly when the problem happened and on which phone.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes to this policy

If the app starts using information differently, we will update this policy and the date at the top before that version of the app is released.

Privacy policy

Weather: Living Sky, by Nikhil. Last updated October 23, 2026.

Weather: Living Sky (“the app”) is an Android weather app with home-screen widgets, made by Nikhil (“we”, “us”). Here is what the app uses, where it goes, and what you can do about it.

The short version

  • No ads, no account, no tracking and no analytics.
  • We don’t collect your data. To get your forecast, the app sends a location (yours, or a city you searched for) straight to the weather services below. We never see it.
  • Your settings and saved forecast stay on your phone.
  • If you send us feedback from Settings, we get your message plus your phone model and app version, so we can look into it.

What the app uses, and why

Your location (only if you allow it)

If you allow location, the app asks Android (through Google Play services) where your phone is, and uses it to:

  • get your forecast: the latitude and longitude, to four decimal places (about 11 metres), go to Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com). For places in Europe, they also go to Open-Meteo’s air-quality service for pollen levels.
  • get official weather alerts: for places in or near the United States, they go to the US National Weather Service (api.weather.gov).
  • name the place: Android’s built-in address lookup turns the location into a town and state. On most phones, Google provides this lookup.

The app never reads your location in the background. Home-screen widgets use the last location the app saw while it was open.

Rather not share your location? Search for a city instead.

City searches

When you search for a city, what you type goes to Open-Meteo’s place search to find matching places. Your last five picked cities stay on your phone so you can pick them again, and “Clear” in the search screen removes them.

Feedback

If you write to us from Settings > Send feedback, we get your message plus your app version, Android version and phone model, so we can look into what you describe. Nothing else comes with it: no location and no city. It travels over HTTPS to a small Google Apps Script in our Google account, which emails it to us at weatherbynikhil@gmail.com. Like any internet request, it reaches Google with your phone’s IP address; we don’t see that address. If you include contact details, we use them only to reply to you. We keep feedback only as long as it helps us improve the app, and we never sell or share it.

What stays on your phone

The app keeps these in its private storage, which other apps can’t read:

  • your settings: units, which forecast values to show, animation choices, each widget’s setup, and whether you have seen the welcome screen;
  • the city you picked, your last five picked cities, and the last device location the app saw (so widgets can refresh without asking for your location again);
  • the most recent forecast, including the place’s name and coordinates, so the app and widgets have something to show offline;
  • pictures of the buildings drawn in the animated scene (no personal information).

Clearing the app’s storage in Android settings, or uninstalling the app, deletes all of it.

Backups. On Android 12 and later, your settings (including the last device location and recent cities) go into your Google backup only when that backup is end-to-end encrypted with your phone’s screen lock, and they are copied when you move straight to a new phone. The saved forecast is never backed up. On Android 11 and earlier, nothing that contains a location is backed up.

Permissions

Location is the only permission the app asks you for, and it’s optional. The rest of what it needs, such as internet access and keeping widgets up to date, Android grants when the app is installed, without asking.

Who receives information

We don’t sell, rent or share your information with anyone. The app talks directly to these services, which also see your phone’s IP address, as with any internet connection:

  • Open-Meteo (Switzerland) receives the coordinates of the place you’re viewing and your city searches. Its terms are at open-meteo.com/en/terms. Open-Meteo says it keeps web server logs, which may contain coordinates, for up to 90 days, and doesn’t link them to identities.
  • The US National Weather Service receives the coordinates of places in or near the United States. Its privacy policy is at weather.gov/privacy.
  • Google, through Google Play services on your phone, handles location requests and the address lookup as part of how Android provides location. Google also carries feedback you send us (Google Apps Script and Gmail). Its privacy policy is at policies.google.com/privacy.
  • We receive only the feedback you choose to send.

If you’ve chosen to share usage and diagnostics with Google on your phone, Google Play may also show us anonymous, combined crash and performance statistics for the app. That is covered by Google’s privacy policy.

Security

Every connection uses HTTPS, and the app refuses unencrypted ones. There is no account or password to steal.

Your choices and rights

  • Allow, limit to “approximate”, or remove location access at any time in Android settings (Settings > Apps > Weather: Living Sky > Permissions).
  • Search for a city instead of using your location.
  • Clear recent searches in the app, or clear all of the app’s data in Android settings.
  • Uninstall the app to remove everything it stored on your phone.

We don’t hold any of your data, so there is nothing to request, export or delete, except feedback you’ve sent us. To have that deleted, write to weatherbynikhil@gmail.com and tell us roughly when you sent it and what it said, so we can find it.

Children

The app isn’t directed at children under 13 and doesn’t knowingly collect information from them.

Changes to this policy

If the app starts using information differently, we’ll update this policy and the date at the top before that version of the app comes out.
